About the course

Data security and privacy is an important part of information security. This course provides an introduction to the fundamental principles and techniques for data security and privacy. The course covers fundamental theories of access control, discretionary access control, mandatory access control, and role-based access control, database access control approaches including grant revoke, virtual private databases and labelled databases, database encryption. On privacy, it covers data anonymization, re-identification attacks, definition, primitives, and applications of differential privacy.

Schedule

One module per week. Weeks 1 to 15 carry new material, week 16 is review. Weeks 8, 9, 12, and 14 release a single lecture rather than two.

Week 1Aug 24
Topic

Overview of the course. What information security means when the adversary is intelligent, and how security goals (confidentiality, integrity, availability) differ from personal privacy. A short history of access control research, from time-sharing systems through multi-level security to role-based models, then the operating system mechanisms underneath it all: CPU privilege modes, the kernel and user space split, memory protection, and system calls as guarded gates.

No deadline
Week 2Aug 31
Topic

Access control as a general security mechanism: subjects, objects, rights, and the access matrix model, and why a system authenticates a human user to a principal rather than to a person. Then UNIX concretely: how directories map names to files, permission bits on files and on directories, the three sets of bits, the process user-ID model with real, effective, and saved IDs, and the suid and sgid bits together with the security problems they introduce.

No deadline
Week 3Sep 7
Topic

Why access control fails with malware. The confused deputy problem. Analysis of DAC weaknesses.

No deadline
Week 4Sep 14
Topic

The Bell-LaPadula model: subjects, objects, the simple security property and the star-property, and the Basic Security Theorem. Why BLP security is not sufficient on its own, which leads to covert channels, and to multi-level security in more detail: security levels, categories, labels, and the need-to-know principle. Closes with what trusted means, the trusted computing base, the reference monitor, and how the TCSEC evaluation divisions tried to assure it.

Quiz 1, HW1 out
Week 5Sep 21
Topic

The Common Criteria and its protection profiles, security targets, and evaluation assurance levels. Then integrity, starting from why it differs from confidentiality: Biba's integrity levels and its five mandatory policies, from strict integrity through the low-water-mark variants to the ring policy, and why integrity requires trusting subjects. Clark-Wilson's two mechanisms for enforcing commercial data integrity and how it departs from BLP, the Chinese Wall policy, and information flow in the non-interference and non-deducibility senses.

No deadline
Week 6Sep 28
Topic

Role-based access control: why roles rather than groups, and the RBAC96 family built up in layers, from RBAC0's users, roles, permissions, and sessions, to RBAC1's role hierarchies, to RBAC2's mutual exclusion and cardinality constraints. Then separation of duties, a critique of the ANSI RBAC standard and whether multiple roles should be activatable at once, and finally attribute-based access control with the XACML policy structure, dataflow model, and rule combining algorithms.

HW1 due
Week 7Oct 5
Topic

k-anonymity, l-diversity, t-closeness. Reconstruction attacks.

HW2 out
Week 8Oct 14
Topic

Definition of epsilon differential privacy. Based on Chapter 2 of the book below. Sensitivity, the Laplace mechanism, and how privacy budget composes across queries.

HW2 due
Week 9Oct 19
Topic

Continue primer on differential privacy: the exponential mechanism for selecting among discrete outputs, and the four settings differential privacy gets applied in, from answering interactive queries to optimizing a single known workload, to non-interactive publishing, to perturbing each record locally before collection.

Quiz 2
Week 10Oct 26
Topic

Definition of LDP. Frequency oracle protocols. Heavy hitter identification and frequent itemset mining under LDP.

HW3 out
Week 11Nov 2
Topic

Publishing histograms under differential privacy: hierarchical methods with their branching factor, constrained inference and mean consistency, budget allocation, and, for spatial data where no good partitioning exists, uniform and adaptive grids and where their error comes from. Then marginal queries on high-dimensional data, where neither the direct nor the flat method works: the PriView approach of choosing views, adding noise, enforcing consistency and non-negativity, then reconstructing. Closes with membership privacy as a general framework and how differential privacy sits inside it.

HW3 due, HW4 out
Week 12Nov 9

Privacy auditing via membership inference: history, current state, and future directions.

Policies

Collaboration

It is allowed and in fact encouraged for students to discuss homework questions. However, you should not look at another student's written or typed answers, and you should not show your written or typed answers to another student. Offences will have consequences ranging from receiving a 0 on the homework to being reported to the school, depending on the seriousness of the offence. Students are encouraged to talk with the instructor about any questions you have about what is permitted on any particular assignment.

Quizzes and both exams are individual work, with no collaboration of any kind. Because they are open book and untimed, this is the policy that matters most in this offering. Please read it carefully.

Use of large language models

You may use LLMs to help you understand the concepts. Copying answers from an LLM is not allowed and is treated as academic dishonesty. When an LLM materially shaped your solution, say so and describe how you used it.

Late policy

You have three extension days for the term, to be used at your discretion across homeworks. The following conditions apply:

  • Use of an extension day must be stated explicitly in the header of the work being turned in.
  • No fractional days.
  • Extension days may not be used to extend submission past the last day of class.

Academic integrity

Academic honesty and ethical behavior are required in this course, as they are in all courses at Purdue University. The class will be conducted according to the policy written by Professor Gene Spafford. Please take the time to read it carefully. This will be followed unless the instructor provides written documentation of exceptions.
