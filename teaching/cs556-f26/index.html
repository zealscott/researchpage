Purdue University, Fall 2026.

This syllabus is a draft and may change before the term begins. Course materials are distributed through Brightspace and are not posted on this page.

About the course

The course covers access control and the formal security models built to reason about it, data privacy from syntactic anonymization through differential privacy in both the central and local models, and the cryptographic primitives these guarantees rest on. The lecture sequence follows the topics listed in the schedule below.

Policies

Collaboration

It is allowed and in fact encouraged for students to discuss homework questions. However, you should not look at another student's written or typed answers, and you should not show your written or typed answers to another student. Offences will have consequences ranging from receiving a 0 on the homework to being reported to the school, depending on the seriousness of the offence. Students are encouraged to talk with the instructor about any questions you have about what is permitted on any particular assignment.

Quizzes and both exams are individual work, with no collaboration of any kind. Because they are open book and untimed, this is the policy that matters most in this offering. Please read it carefully.

Use of large language models

You may use LLMs to help you understand the concepts. Copying answers from an LLM is not allowed and is treated as academic dishonesty. When an LLM materially shaped your solution, say so and describe how you used it.

Late work

You have three late days for the term, to spend across homeworks as you like. A late day counts as a whole day, and once they are gone, late homework is not graded. Late days may not be applied to quizzes or to either exam.

Academic integrity

Academic honesty and ethical behavior are required in this course, as they are in all courses at Purdue University. The class will be conducted according to the policy written by Professor Gene Spafford. Please take the time to read it carefully. This will be followed unless the instructor provides written documentation of exceptions.

Accommodations

Students who need accommodations should contact the Disability Resource Center and share their accommodation letter with me as early in the term as possible.
